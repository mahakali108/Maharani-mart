# Grievance Redressal Policy — review draft

**Version:** 0.1-review-draft
**Prepared:** 30 October 2026
**Status:** **UNPUBLISHED — officer, applicability and workflow approval required**

## 1. Purpose and applicability

This draft describes a formal grievance route for complaints concerning orders, delivery, products, seller conduct, invoices, privacy/data requests, account access and platform conduct. A lawyer must confirm applicability of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, Information Technology Rules, 2021 and any intermediary classification to the actual business model.

## 2. Grievance Officer and contacts

Before publication, the owner must record and verify the Grievance Officer’s name, designation, business address, telephone number, email address, operating entity and approved contact hours. These details must never be replaced by sample/placeholder contact data.

## 3. Complaint intake and reference

A complaint should be accepted through the approved authenticated support/grievance route and, where approved, the verified public contact channel. The system must generate a unique complaint reference and record the received timestamp, complainant/account verification state, subject/category, evidence, assigned reviewer and communication history.

## 4. Status workflow

The intended controlled statuses are:

1. **Received** — complaint accepted and reference issued.
2. **Acknowledged** — acknowledgement recorded with timestamp and responsible team/officer.
3. **Assigned** — an authorised reviewer/owner is assigned.
4. **In progress** — review, evidence collection or operational remediation is underway.
5. **Resolved** — outcome and response are recorded.

Reopening, rejection, withdrawal and escalation rules must be approved and auditable. An uploaded document or filled field is not a resolution by itself.

## 5. Timelines and overdue escalation

The system should support a configurable 24-hour acknowledgement target and 15-day resolution target for legal review where applicable. These are workflow targets only until the owner confirms staff coverage, the lawyer confirms applicability and the process is tested. The final public policy must not represent a target as an operational SLA before that approval.

Overdue complaints should be visibly escalated to authorised Super Admin reviewers with an audit record. A complaint must not silently disappear because an operational target elapsed.

## 6. Evidence, privacy and communications

Only evidence needed to assess the complaint should be collected. Complaint attachments, contact details, order data, seller KYC, bank and identity documents require access controls and retention handling. Communications should be timestamped and attributable. The final privacy policy and retention schedule govern storage/deletion exceptions.

## 7. External escalation and no waiver

The final wording must preserve rights and external remedies that apply by law. It must not impose an unlawful exclusive forum, payment or waiver condition. Lawyer-approved governing-law and jurisdiction text belongs in the final terms/policy set.

## Publication approvals required

- Owner: named officer, verified contact details, staffing/escalation process and evidence storage.
- Lawyer: regulatory applicability, timeline wording, rights/escalation and publication text.
- Security/privacy reviewer: access, retention and incident handling.
